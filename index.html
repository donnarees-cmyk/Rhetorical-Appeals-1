Worked example: one speech, three appeals

As a mother of three, I have felt the heartbreak of a child going to bed hungry. One in five kids in our town does not get enough to eat, so we must hold a food drive.

Ethos: her experience as a parent builds trust Pathos: "heartbreak" and "hungry child" create feeling Logos: a statistic gives proof

Step 7 · Cheat Sheet

🎓 Ethos
credibility, ethics		Can I trust this person?experience, job title, experts, "it's the right thing"
❤️ Pathos
emotion		How does this make me feel?"Imagine…", feeling words, vivid images, sad or happy stories
🧠 Logos

3 steps to identify an appeal

  1. Read the sentence.
  2. Ask the question: trust? feeling? proof?
  3. Prove it with a clue from the text.

Sentence frame for explaining

The author uses [ethos / pathos / logos] when they write "[quote the clue]." This appeals to the reader's [trust / feelings / reason] because [explain].

Remember: one sentence can use more than one appeal, and an author's goal is always to persuade.

Teacher note: ELA.6.R.3.4 clarification: students identify the appeals of logos, ethos, and pathos. Rhetorical devices come in later grades.
